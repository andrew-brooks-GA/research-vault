## Issue 7. Change-diff on refresh: show what changed, not just that it changed

Original ask, in summary: on a hash mismatch, show a textual diff between the captured content and the fetched content, optionally have the agent summarise the delta, then offer confirm or supersede; the issue noted that capture stores a hash, not necessarily the body, and that this touches the hash-only stance in `SECURITY.md` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault).

What exists now. `refresh` fetches each target and reports `unreachable` on a status of 400 or more, `confirmed` when the hash matches, and otherwise `changed` with the detail `run verify`; the word `diff` does not occur in `refresh.mjs` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `refresh.mjs` lines 35 to 46 and its grep). Its `run` reads `--vault`, `--id` and `--dry-run`, prints one text line per result, and has no `--json`; the targets are manifest rows, which carry no body (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `refresh.mjs` lines 19 to 28 and 50 to 66, REFERENCE line 189). `SECURITY.md` says `refresh` is hash-only, never stores the fetched bytes and never mutates an entry (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `SECURITY.md` lines 31 to 43). The stored side of a diff exists since 0.2.0: `capture --store-body` with `--ack-data-egress` appends the given content to the source's body after the optional skeleton, and the CLI passes both flags (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `capture.mjs` lines 27, 104 to 112 and 211, CHANGELOG line 290). Nothing marks that an entry holds stored text; `advise` has an `isStubBody` test, a body with no line other than blanks, a lone `-`, a heading or a bare fence, which its stub list applies to every type except `source` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `advise.mjs` lines 9 to 18). The fetched side does not exist: `fetchSource`'s response handler resolves `{ status, hash }` with no body (2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-rescoping, `fetchsource.mjs` lines 50 to 67). Capture does no text extraction: the content it hashes and stores is what the caller passes in `content` or `content-file` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `capture.mjs` lines 34 and 35; 2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-rescoping, line 203).

What remains, narrowed to the route that keeps `SECURITY.md` as it is: the diff is the agent's step, not `refresh`'s. `refresh` gains `--json`, one object per result, `{ id, result, detail?, storedBody }`, where `storedBody` is `isStubBody` negated over the entry file's body, read for `changed` results only. The research-verify skill, on a `changed` entry with `storedBody` true, fetches the page as the agent does for any verification, diffs the extracted text against the stored body, summarises the delta in the verification's `notes`, and then confirms or supersedes. A `refresh --diff` that keeps fetched bytes in memory is the other route; it changes the hash-only contract and compares the raw fetched bytes with whatever text the caller stored, so it is not proposed. Done when `refresh --json` prints the four fields and the skill names the step and the field it keys on.

## Issue 8. Contradiction / stale-supersede detection (advisory)

Original ask, in summary: advisory checks, outside `lint --check`, for two live entries on one subject whose claims conflict, for a confirmed entry a newer sibling contradicts without a `superseded_by`, and for supersede suspects, a newer entry on the same subject with the older never marked superseded (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault).

What exists now. Lint's one rule naming supersession is structural: an entry with `status: superseded` and no `superseded_by` is a violation, and it is the file's only line naming supersede (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `lintrules.mjs` line 139 and its `supersede` grep). `verify --result outdated` requires `--superseded-by` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `verify.mjs` lines 28 to 30), and every `verify` call pushes one verification row, and `--succession` then bumps `updated`, keeps the entry active and writes no supersede, while `--result outdated` sets `status: superseded` and `superseded_by` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `verify.mjs` lines 17 to 60). The research-verify skill says a newer product version is not a supersede: the older entry stays `active` and the new version is captured as a sibling sharing the `series` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, research-verify skill line 11). Staleness is computed per entry from its volatility window and `last_verified` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `stale.mjs` lines 10 to 17); `advise`'s one cross-entry freshness check, `staleEvidence`, follows `sources` and `contributing_ids` edges, not sibling entries on one subject (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `advise.mjs` lines 51 to 79). `advise` has no sibling or contradiction key (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `advise.mjs` lines 81 to 100). The manifest row carries `subject`, `series`, `source_url`, `status` and `superseded_by` but no creation date; the id's date prefix is the entry's date (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `manifest.mjs` lines 28 to 46, `capture.mjs` line 52).

What remains, split by what can be computed. A `supersedeSuspects` key in `advise` lists pairs of non-superseded entries that claim the same thing twice: the same subject name with the same version, or no version on either, or the same `source_url`; newer is the later id prefix. Siblings with different versions are the succession the verify skill prescribes and are not listed. A pair leaves the list when one entry is superseded, or when the two are given the distinct versions they carry. The semantic checks, two live entries whose claims conflict, stay with the agent: one step in the research-librarian skill, which already reads `advise`, that takes the suspects list and judges each pair. Nothing enters `lint --check`. Done when `advise` lists suspects with the field that paired them, the librarian skill has the step, and the skill's section list and REFERENCE's `advise` row name the new section.

## Issue 9. Authority + freshness ranking in search

Original ask, in summary: rank or annotate search results by `authority_tier`, freshness within the volatility window and text relevance, show the signals inline, and add `--sort authority|fresh|relevance` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault).

What exists now. `search` filters manifest rows by type, domain, series, topic through the aliases, text over title, topics and summary, and optionally body, then prints them in manifest order, which is the sorted directory walk; none of `sort`, `rank`, `authority` or `last_verified` occurs in `search.mjs` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `search.mjs` lines 1 to 45 and its grep, `manifest.mjs` lines 24 to 30, `fsutil.mjs` line 14). The signals are on the manifest row: `authority_tier`, `authority_basis`, `volatility` and `last_verified` (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `manifest.mjs` lines 28 to 46). The tier is a source field: the frontmatter schema lists `authority_tier` and `authority_basis` under `source` and not under `note`, `synthesis` or `question`, and `capture` sets them in its source branch only, so other types are expected to carry none and their row value is null (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `frontmatter.schema.json` lines 5 and 6, `capture.mjs` lines 67 to 76; 2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-rescoping, `frontmatter.schema.json` lines 7 and 10). The tier order is the array in the plugin taxonomy, `primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary`, and `authority_tier` is not among the vocabularies a vault may extend (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `taxonomy.json` line 27, `projectconfig.mjs` lines 15 to 22). `staleness()` in `stale.mjs` turns volatility and `last_verified` into fresh, stale or not assessable (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `stale.mjs` lines 10 to 17).

What remains: a `--sort authority|fresh` option on `search`. `authority` orders by the taxonomy array and puts rows with no tier after the sources in their manifest order; `fresh` orders by `staleness()` with not-assessable rows last. The current order stays the default. Each printed row gains an annotation, the tier or `no tier`, and `fresh`, `stale` or nothing, so the choice is informed even unsorted. Text relevance beyond the substring match waits for a need. Done when the two sorts and the annotation exist and the default output is unchanged apart from the annotation.

## Issue 14. RAG-ready export: chunked records with metadata for embedding pipelines

Original ask, in summary: an export mode emitting chunked text records with parent id, topics, authority tier, `last_verified`, volatility and source URL, sized by character or token windows with overlap, with a filter to fresh, non-superseded entries above an authority floor; zero-dependency, writing outside the vault only (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault).

What exists now. `export` emits at most one record per entry in scope, the scope defaulting to `question`: a question only when answered with a non-empty `answer_summary`, as `{ input: question, output: answer_summary }`, any other type `{ input: title, output: body or summary }`, each with `meta` of `id`, `type` and `last_verified`, sorted by id; superseded entries are not filtered; the only format is `jsonl`; bodies need both `--include-bodies` and `--ack-data-egress`; `--out` must lie outside the vault (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `exportjsonl.mjs` lines 1 to 30, `export.mjs` lines 1 to 30, `SECURITY.md` lines 45 to 53). The word `chunk` does not occur in either export file (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault). The metadata the issue lists is on the manifest row: `topics`, `authority_tier`, `last_verified`, `volatility`, `source_url`, with the tier a source field that other types are expected to lack (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `manifest.mjs` lines 28 to 46, `frontmatter.schema.json` lines 5 and 6).

What remains: `export --chunk <chars> --overlap <chars>`, emitting one record per window with the parent id, the listed metadata and the window's offset, under the same body gate and the same `--out` rule; `--fresh-only`, which drops entries `staleness()` reports stale and entries with `status: superseded`; and `--min-authority <tier>`, which drops sources below the tier and passes rows with no tier through. Token windows are out: `package.json` declares no dependencies and nothing in `bin` names a tokenizer (no `tokeniz` or `tiktoken` match), the plugin will not add one, and characters are enough for a pipeline that embeds (2026-11-02-nine-remaining-issues-at-research-vault, `package.json` and the `tokeniz` grep). Done when a chunked export round-trips through a reader that reassembles an entry from its windows, `--fresh-only` drops a stale and a superseded entry, and `--min-authority` drops a tertiary source and keeps a question.

## The order of the twelve

Ranked by benefit to this project against the size of the change, highest first. The prefix is the number the issue's title gets.

1. #9, search ranking. The signal fields are on every manifest row (the tier is null on a source captured without `--authority-tier`) and `staleness()` exists; one option and one annotation address the citation-choice failure the vault exists to prevent.
2. #13, topic alias normaliser. Three small edits: an extension key, two `vaultPath` arguments, one rewrite in `fixVault`; every `search` command and the teach path pool go through the same `matchRows`.
3. #12, question lifecycle. One `advise` key and one close command; open questions are the project's forgotten threads.
4. #8, supersede suspects. A manifest-only pairing in `advise` plus one skill step; it adds a per-subject check beside the per-entry and per-edge freshness checks that exist.
5. #7, refresh diff. One output flag and skill text; it makes a `changed` result on a source with stored text actionable without touching the network contract.
6. #11, near-duplicates. Advisory pairing in `advise`; stdlib string work; the resolution is the existing supersede.
7. #15, citation log. Hook-based, moderate; it is the prerequisite for #6 and the first usage data the vault will have.
8. #6, hit-rate report. A report over #15; cheap once #15 exists, nothing before.
9. #10, bulk ingest. The fetch gives `capture` its first network path and a `SECURITY.md` change; the orchestrate skill already fetches and captures many sources per run, one capture per URL, and batches the distillation entries, so the gain is the reading-list case alone.
10. #5, source blocklist. Cheap, but in my judgment the need has not shown up in this vault.
11. #14, chunked export. In my judgment it serves an embedding pipeline this project does not run; the records are easy, the consumer is elsewhere.
12. #16, semantic search. An opt-in layer on #14 and #9, a second outbound path, furthest from the zero-dependency core.
