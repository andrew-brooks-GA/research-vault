## Problems

1. **One shape for every lesson.** LESSON-FORMAT.md's Blocks table requires, for a lesson, `mission`, `model`, `fact`, `example`, `quiz`, `step`, `sources` and `followup`, in that order; a review lesson drops `model`, `fact`, `example` and `step`; nothing else varies (2026-11-01-pedagogy-spec-lines-at-research-vault). The four vcluster lessons all have the same nine blocks in the same order, one figure each, one `step` block each and 4 or 5 quiz items each (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose). A lesson that teaches a concept (lesson 0001, two control planes), one that teaches a tool (lesson 0003, the CLI) and one that teaches a diagnosis (lesson 0004, which side is it on) are poured into the same mould. The inherited skill asks for "a single tangible win" per lesson and that a lesson be "completable very quickly" because "working memory is very small" (2026-11-01-matt-pocock-teach-skill-skill-md); the block list is this repo's rendering of that advice, not something the source skill prescribes.
2. **The split rule counts things; it does not weigh them.** A lesson introduces at most three terms or one tool (LESSON-FORMAT.md Terms, Commands and tools), a path line with both `terms:` and `tool:` "is two lessons" (PATH-FORMAT.md Rules), and a lesson that needs a third reader round "is the wrong lesson (too much in it), so split it" (SKILL.md §5), all quoted in 2026-11-01-pedagogy-spec-lines-at-research-vault. None of these says what makes a lesson too much: the Terms section itself says "There is no word cap: the cap is on new ideas." The measured lessons grew from 1421 to 2743 prose words and the `fact` block from 163 to 900 words between lessons 0001 and 0004 (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose), while `teach lint` is clean on the workspace and each lesson declares at most two terms or one tool (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first). The cognitive-load literature names the quantity that matters, element interactivity, and says it depends on the material and on the learner together (D2).
3. **One figure must carry the whole model, and the page squashes it.** The model block "draws the parts once in a `<figure>`" (LESSON-FORMAT.md, The model block) and `teach lint` reports `LESSON_MODEL_NO_FIGURE` for a model with none (bin/lib/lesson.mjs line 305), both quoted in 2026-11-01-pedagogy-spec-lines-at-research-vault. Lesson 0004's one figure holds 31 labels of 125 words, the longest 85 characters, at font-size 11 and 12 in a 680 by 330 viewBox, up from 14 labels in lesson 0001 (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose). The SVG carries `width="680" height="330"` attributes (its open tag is quoted in the snippet) and the stylesheet gives it `max-width: 100%; height: auto`, so by arithmetic over the stylesheet it is capped at 680 CSS px wide on wide screens while the model block's text runs about 1331 px wide, and on a 375 px viewport the figure scales by 0.38 so an 11-unit label renders at about 4 px (2026-11-01-estimated-rendered-line-length-about-150). The model prose that the figure should sit beside is 665 words in two paragraphs (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose).
4. **No whole task ever.** Every `PATH.md` line is one new thing and the only other line kind is a review every six or seven lessons (PATH-FORMAT.md Rules, quoted); a review lesson is a quiz over earlier records (LESSON-FORMAT.md Review lessons, quoted). The `step` block is "One real-world action, the command to run, and the observable result" (Blocks table). Nothing in the path asks the learner to do something that needs several lessons at once with the solution withheld. The Zig book the user named is built the other way round: four project chapters sit among thirteen topic chapters, the author says it "explores the exciting world of Zig through small and simple projects", and Project 1 opens with the goal, explains the algorithm first, builds from one core piece and links the full source file rather than printing it ("If you are an anxious person, and you want to see now the full source code ...") (2026-10-22-introduction-zig-pedro-duarte-faria-book; 2026-11-01-faria-introduction-zig-chapter-4-project). D3 takes the concept, whole tasks on a fading scaffold, not the book's chapter plan.
5. **The quiz is the only retrieval, and its rules are about typography.** The quiz has at least 3 items of 3 or 4 options each, options within a 1.6 length spread, and the widget shuffles them (LESSON-FORMAT.md Quiz data, quoted); the inherited rule is that answers be "exactly the same number of words" (2026-11-01-matt-pocock-teach-skill-skill-md). Nothing says what a wrong option must be for the item to teach, and nothing varies the retrieval format with what the lesson teaches. The evidence on what makes a multiple-choice item productive, and what feedback does to the lures, is in D5.
6. **The page runs far past the reading guidance it could meet for free.** With `body { max-width: 90rem }` and the `[data-block]` padding quoted in the snippet, a full-width block's text column is about 1331 px, about 151 to 163 characters per line at 17 px by arithmetic (2026-11-01-estimated-rendered-line-length-about-150). WCAG SC 1.4.8 lists "Width is no more than 80 characters or glyphs (40 if CJK)." among the visual-presentation mechanisms a user must be able to reach, with the note "Content is not required to use these values." (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-visual-presentation-understanding-sc); Baymard's research summary gives "The optimal line length for body text is 50–75 characters" and the fix, "the CSS max-width property can be set using font-relative lengths of around 70ch or 34em" (2026-11-01-scott-2022-readability-optimal-line-length); Butterick's range is "45–90 characters, including spaces" (2026-11-01-butterick-line-length-butterick-s-practical). Every block also carries a label (`the model`, `what the lab showed`, `the output, read with me`; the `::before` rules are quoted in the snippet) and, on an unserved page, every citation an inline chip, while a served page numbers them `[n]` (lesson.css, quoted in the snippet); whether these are signals or clutter is a design question D6 answers from the captured guidance rather than taste.

## Design

Each decision names the evidence it rests on and the smallest change that carries it. The notes are today's distillations; each lists its sources.

### D0. Contracts kept

1. The vault, the probe, the reader gate and the claims gate are unchanged. A project's reference solution and a `predict` item's answer are claims and go through the same gates as a `fact` paragraph.
2. `teach lint` stays the enforcement point for structure. The judgements this spec adds to `PLAN.md` (the Elements part, the reason for a scaffold) are the author's, recorded for the session and for the next author; no gate reads `PLAN.md` (the claims reviewer reads "only the draft and those entries", the lesson reader "only that file"; both agent files are quoted in 2026-11-01-pedagogy-spec-lines-at-research-vault), and this spec does not claim otherwise.
3. Existing lessons stay valid. A lesson with no `data-shape` is shape `concept` with today's block list; a figure outside a pair spans the block, and D4.5 says how the four existing figures render until they are redrawn.
4. ESM only, no dependencies, no classes. Three `COUPLINGS` rows, since each pair depends on a shared value rather than a path (`AGENTS.md` Rules, quoted): the shape table (format file, `bin/lib/lesson.mjs`, the `test/fixtures/teach` workspace), the figure constants (format file, lint, `lesson.css`), the block label list (format file, `lesson.css`).

### D1. Shapes: a fixed frame, a varying middle

A lesson declares `<body data-shape="concept|tool|diagnose|project">`; review lessons (`lessons/NNNN-review-slug.html`, LESSON-FORMAT.md, quoted) keep their block list. The frame is the same on every page: `prereq` (optional), `mission`, the shape's middle, `sources`, `followup`. `mission` keeps its label `why this lesson` and gains a second sentence naming the activity ("you will complete the command yourself", "you will read the output before the explanation", "you will build it whole"); `followup` is preceded by one sentence stating the win now held. Only the middle varies:

| Shape | The win | Middle blocks, in order | The retrieval it must contain |
|---|---|---|---|
| `concept` | a term set and the model behind it | `model`, `fact`, `quiz`, `connect`; `example` and `step` optional | a quiz whose wrong options are nameable misreadings (D5.1), then `connect` |
| `tool` | one tool: what it is, one command, how to read its output | `model`, `example`, `complete`, `step`; `quiz` optional | `complete`, the worked command with one part withheld, then the unaided `step` |
| `diagnose` | reading output and deciding what it means | `predict`, `model`, `fact`, `quiz`; `step` optional | `predict`, one question on the output before the explanation; quiz lures are the misreadings |
| `project` | a whole task that needs several earlier lessons at once | `scaffold` (D3), `step` | the task itself; `step` is the check command and its expected output, and the learner's run is the attempt |

Why a frame: consistency. "Consistent sequences of actions should be required in similar situations; identical terminology should be used in prompts, menus, and help screens" (2026-11-01-shneiderman-2016-eight-golden-rules-interface) and "The user should not have to remember information from one part of the interface to another." (2026-11-01-nielsen-2020-10-usability-heuristics-user); the learner meets the same top and bottom on every page and one named activity in the middle.

Why the middle varies: the guidance that helps most depends on what is being learned and on what the learner holds. For a novice at a procedure, "studying worked examples seems invariably superior to discovering or constructing a solution to a problem" and the effect "first disappears and then reverses as the learners’ expertise increases" (2026-11-01-kirschner-sweller-clark-2006-why-minimal). So `tool` is a worked example (`example`) followed by a completion item (`complete`) and an unaided run (`step`); the completion item is the 4C/ID form "students receive partially-completed applications they must finish (i.e., completion tasks)" (2026-11-01-frerejean-van-merrienboer-kirschner-roex-aertgeerts), and the fade continues across later lessons, where the tool's commands appear unaided (D3). The direction, complete example to problem, is Renkl, Atkinson and Maier's finding that "the fading procedure fosters learning, at least when near transfer performance is considered" (2026-11-01-renkl-atkinson-maier-2000-from-example); their fade ran over four tasks, three examples and then a problem, which is why this spec does not call one withheld part a fading sequence. `diagnose` opens with a question because of the pretesting effect: "Posttest performance was better in the test condition than in the extended study condition in all experiments—a pretesting effect— even though only items that were not successfully retrieved on the pretest were analyzed." (2026-11-01-richland-kornell-kao-2009-pretesting-effect). That evidence is a short pretest before reading (five questions in Richland et al.); `predict` asks one, an extrapolation this spec makes knowingly, and it is not a problem-solving phase, and this spec does not cite productive failure for it (Sinha and Kapur's pooled effect is for conceptual knowledge and transfer and "a nonsignificant effect (Hedge’s g) of −0.03" for procedural knowledge, 2026-09-12-when-problem-solving-followed-by-instruction, which is one more reason `predict` never appears in `tool`). `connect` is a terminal prompt, not a widget item: the learner says how the new term relates to two terms they hold, the skill judges it in conversation and records an understanding record when it holds (`teach record add --kind understanding`, SKILL.md §6, quoted in the snippet). The reason is the generative activity principle, "People learn better when they are guided in" / "carrying out generative learning activities during learning." (2026-11-01-mayer-2020-multimedia-principles-one-page), with the concept-map evidence read for its direction only, the learner constructing the link beats being shown it (2026-11-01-concept-maps-help-when-learner-constructs).

Lint: `LESSON_SHAPE_UNKNOWN` for a value outside the table; `LESSON_SHAPE_BLOCK_MISSING` replaces today's `LESSON_BLOCK_MISSING` over the `REQUIRED` table (bin/lib/lesson.mjs lines 17 and 276, quoted in the snippet) and reads one `SHAPES` table; a `tool` lesson without `data-teaches`, or any other shape with it, is `LESSON_SHAPE_MISMATCH`. `complete` holds one `<pre>` command with `<mark aria-label="withheld">____</mark>` over the withheld part and one `<pre>` expected output; the command with the mark filled must equal a command the `example` block ran (`LESSON_COMPLETE_NOT_WORKED`). `predict` holds a `<pre>` output and one quiz item (`data-quiz`, one item) whose options use only terms allowed before this lesson's `data-introduces`; the widget merges it into the lesson's one attempt as item 0, so `/progress` shows one score. Four new block names, `complete`, `predict`, `connect`, `scaffold`, each with a label and a colour in `lesson.css` (D6.3); the project reuses `mission`, `prereq`, `step` and `sources`.

### D2. The split and merge rule: elements held at once, written in the plan

`PLAN.md` gains a sixth part after the five LESSON-FORMAT.md names (the win, the fact, the claims, the probe, the step; "Plan before probe", quoted in the snippet), **Elements**: the parts the learner must hold at once to understand this win, each marked `held` (a Floor term, an earlier lesson's term or tool, a record) or `new`. A lesson adds one `new` structure. The split test is the isolated-elements effect: when a paragraph would have to introduce a part and its interaction with other new parts in the same breath, the parts come first in their own lesson and the interaction after: "a sequence of only presenting the individual elements to learners followed by all of the information including both the individual elements and their interactions would be superior to presenting all of the information twice. Results supported this hypothesis." (2026-11-01-sweller-van-merri-nboer-paas-2019). The merge test is its converse: two adjacent path lines that each add one element to the same structure, neither teaching a tool, are one lesson. The count is relative to the learner, "Complexity or element interactivity depends on a combination of both the nature of the information and the knowledge of the person processing the information" (same source), which is why the plan, which reads the records, decides; the lint checks only that the part exists (`LESSON_PLAN_NO_ELEMENTS`, beside the existing finding for an `evidence/NNNN/` folder with no `PLAN.md`, quoted in the snippet). The `fact` block states claims about `new` elements; a paragraph restating a `held` element becomes a link to the lesson that taught it, which is where the measured growth of `fact` from 163 to 900 words (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose) is addressed.

The terms and tool caps stay (they bound new ideas). No word or time cap is added: the only length evidence captured is engagement data on video, "median engagement time is at most 6 minutes, regardless of total video length", whose authors say "it is important not to draw any conclusions about student learning solely from our findings about video engagement" (2026-11-01-guo-kim-rubin-2014-how-video). The page is learner-paced, which is what segmenting asks ("presented in user-paced segments rather than as continuous unit", 2026-11-01-mayer-2020-multimedia-principles-one-page); D4 makes the segments visible. SKILL.md §5's reader-round rule is left to spec 21, a sibling session's draft whose P4 proposes its rewording (quoted in the snippet as read today).

### D3. Projects: whole tasks on the path, support fading across the mission's projects

`PATH.md` gains a line kind: a title starting `Project:` with `after:` naming at least two lines (`PATH_PROJECT_AFTER` otherwise). The skill's §2 writes one at the end of each group of two to five lines that together build one capability, so the path reads as groups closed by a project, with reviews as today; later projects integrate more lines, so the projects run simple to complex across the mission. `teach next` treats a `Project:` line like a review line in the inner-fringe rule, "the built non-review lines that no other built non-review line names in `after:`" (PATH-FORMAT.md, quoted), so a built project does not remove the lessons it names from the inner fringe, and `teach path edges` skips `Project:` lines (a project uses every term it integrates; proposing those as `after:` would only restate its group). The `fringe:` rule is unchanged.

A project lesson's `mission` states the goal as the mission criterion it serves and the observable result that proves it; `prereq` names the lessons it assumes and nothing else; `step` is the check command whose output proves the goal, stated exactly as the reference run's Result quotes it, so the claims gate can match it; `sources` carries the reference solution as a link to its snippet, not printed on the page. The mission's first project has a `scaffold` block, a completion task in the `complete` form over the solution; later projects have none unless the plan says why. The learner's run is the attempt: `teach attempt add --lesson <id> --result pass|fail` (a new form; `--answers` is unchanged), and a `fail` is a corrected-misconception candidate for the records the project draws on.

Evidence. Whole tasks as the spine: "Learning tasks are treated as the backbone of an educational program" (2026-11-01-4cid-org-about-4c-id-model) and "Learning tasks of equal complexity are grouped together, creating task classes that are sequenced from simple to complex." (2026-11-01-frerejean-van-merrienboer-kirschner-roex-aertgeerts). The fade from a scaffolded first project to unaided later ones: "In a process of scaffolding, this support and guidance are gradually withdrawn until the learners are able to independently carry out tasks of a certain level of complexity" (same source) and the expertise reversal effect, "instructional guidance should be provided at the appropriate time, while unnecessary support removed as learners progress to more advanced levels of proficiency in a specific task domain" (2026-11-01-kalyuga-2007-expertise-reversal-effect-its). Doing: "the learning benefit from extra doing (1 SD increase) to be more than six times that of extra watching or reading", from data that "adds uncertainty in making inferences about causal relationships" (2026-11-01-koedinger-kim-jia-mclaughlin-bier-2015). The page shape of a project, goal first, explanation before code, the full solution one link away, is what the Zig book's first project does ("As our first small project, I want to implement a base64 encoder/decoder with you."; "you can find it at the ZigExamples folder in the official repository of this book", 2026-11-01-faria-introduction-zig-chapter-4-project); the concept is taken, the chapter plan is not (2026-11-01-whole-tasks-backbone-sequence-them-simple). What this is not: 4C/ID starts from whole tasks and teaches parts around them; this path teaches parts and closes each group with a whole task, which is a capstone, and the spec says so.

### D4. Figures: used-together stays together, labels name, the viewBox sets the size

1. **One figure per idea, and the comparison stays whole.** The model block may hold several figures, each in a pair with the paragraph it illustrates: `<div class="pair"><p>…</p><figure>…</figure></div>`. The test for splitting a figure is Larkin and Simon's: "Diagrams can group together all information that is used together, thus avoiding large amounts of search for the elements needed to make a problem-solving inference." (2026-11-01-larkin-simon-1987-why-diagram-sometimes). Parts the prose uses separately get their own small figure; parts the learner must compare (lesson 0004's figure describes itself as "Left, the host cluster vc-lesson" against "Right, the virtual side", 2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first) stay in one figure. Words beside pictures: "People learn better when corresponding words and" / "pictures are presented near rather than far from each other on the page or screen." (2026-11-01-mayer-2020-multimedia-principles-one-page). `LESSON_MODEL_NO_FIGURE` stays.
2. **Labels name; the prose explains.** Larkin and Simon's criterion "Minimal labeling. The only important functions of labels in Figure 5 is to relate parts of the real-world diagram unambiguously to the force diagram." and "The labels in Figure 6 serve only to let us talk about the elements there. They are not essential to any of the inferences made." (same source) is the rule: a label is a name, at most 40 characters including its `<tspan>`s (`WARN_FIGURE_LABEL_LONG`). No label-count budget: a count would need a working-memory claim, and the figure is external memory, which is the point of using one. Lesson 0004's longest label, 85 characters (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose), becomes a name with the sentence moved to the paragraph beside the figure.
3. **Size from the viewBox, one authoring width.** The `<svg>` carries `viewBox` and no `width` or `height` attribute (`WARN_FIGURE_FIXED_SIZE`); the viewBox width is at most 440 units (`WARN_FIGURE_VIEWBOX_WIDE`); every label is at least 14 units (`WARN_FIGURE_LABEL_SMALL`, resolved through the nearest `font-size` attribute on the text or an ancestor; a `style` or class is unknown and warns). The stylesheet sets `figure svg { display: block; width: 100%; height: auto; min-width: 27.5rem }` and `figure { overflow-x: auto }`, and drops today's `max-width: 100%`. The `width` attribute is "The displayed width of the rectangular viewport. (Not the width of its coordinate system.)" (2026-11-01-mdn-svg-svg-element-reference), which is why today's `width="680"` caps the figure at 680 px beside 1331 px of text (2026-11-01-estimated-rendered-line-length-about-150); with the viewBox alone, "a transform stretches or resizes the SVG viewport to fit a particular container element" (2026-11-01-mdn-viewbox-svg-attribute-reference). Since "one user unit corresponds to one CSS pixel" at scale one (2026-11-01-mdn-positions-svg-from-scratch-tutorial), by arithmetic a 440-unit figure at a 27.5rem (440 px) minimum is at scale 1.00 and a 14-unit label renders at 14 px (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first); on a phone the figure scrolls sideways instead, which WCAG 1.4.10 allows for "images required for understanding (such as maps and diagrams)" (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-understanding-sc-1-4). The constants 440, 27.5rem and 14 are defaults: by the same arithmetic a figure in a pair has about 449 px at the 1100 px breakpoint and 619 px at the 90rem cap, so 440 units scale by 1.02 and 1.41 there (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first), and 14 units is a choice just above the figcaption's 0.85rem (13.6 px), not a sourced minimum; none is a measured optimum, and `@media print { figure { overflow: visible } figure svg { min-width: 0 } }` is meant to keep a figure on the page (verified in print preview at implementation).
4. **Colour and text alternative.** Graphical parts use `currentColor` or `--muted` only (`WARN_FIGURE_COLOR` for any other fill or stroke); by the WCAG luminance formula over the tokens quoted in the snippet, `--muted` is 6.13:1 and 5.41:1 on the light card and code backgrounds and 6.77:1 and 7.09:1 on the dark ones, while `--rule` is 1.11:1 to 1.40:1 (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first), so `--rule` is decoration only: "Parts of graphics required to understand the content" need 3:1 and "the computed values should not be rounded (e.g. 2.999:1 would not meet the 3:1 threshold)." (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-non-text-contrast-understanding). Each `<svg>` carries `role="img"` and its own `aria-label` (`WARN_FIGURE_NO_ALT`), so splitting lesson 0004's one figure also splits its one long description.
5. **Existing lessons.** A figure outside a pair spans the block and is sized by the new rules. By arithmetic, lesson 0004's 680-unit figure spanning the model block at the 90rem cap is at scale 1.90 with 11-unit labels near 21 px; on a 375 px viewport the 440 px minimum leaves it at scale 0.65 with labels near 7 px (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first), which is better than today's 4 px and still too small, so the four legacy figures are redrawn under the cap when their lessons are next touched. On lesson 0004 the fixed-size, viewBox-width, label-size and label-length warnings would fire (its open tag, sizes and longest label are recorded; its fills and strokes are `currentColor` or `none` and it carries `role="img"`, so the colour and alternative-text warnings would not), and all are advisory.

### D5. Retrieval: competitive lures, three options, feedback kept, the gap stays

1. **Every wrong option is a nameable misreading.** LESSON-FORMAT.md's quiz rules gain: each wrong option is an answer a learner with a stated misreading would give, and its `why` names that misreading. No lint; the rule is author-side, beside the existing hygiene rules. Multiple choice teaches only this way: "they must include plausible (i.e., competitive) incorrect alternatives of the kind that can trigger the retrieval processes that foster test-induced learning" (2026-11-01-little-bjork-bjork-angello-2012-multiple); "competitive alternatives are necessary for this benefit to occur" (2026-11-01-little-bjork-2015-optimizing-multiple-choice).
2. **Three options by default; the 3-item floor stays.** The 1.6 length spread, equal formatting and shuffling stay. Three options is the default because "a greater number of alternatives led to a higher proportion of intrusions" (2026-11-01-butler-roediger-2008-feedback-enhances-positive), an effect on intrusions that feedback largely removes, and because the cited benefit was shown with "one correct alternative and two incorrect alternatives" (2026-11-01-little-bjork-2015-optimizing-multiple-choice). Four stays allowed.
3. **No typed items.** Exact-match text items were considered and dropped: trimmed, lower-cased matching is fill-in-the-blank, which the review ranks with recognition among the "less generative responses" (2026-11-01-dunlosky-rawson-marsh-nathan-willingham-2013). Generation happens in `complete`, `step`, `connect` and the project, where the learner produces a command or a sentence and the skill or the run judges it.
4. **Feedback stays immediate**, since both immediate and delayed feedback "increased the proportion of correct responses and reduced the proportion of intrusions" over none (2026-11-01-butler-roediger-2008-feedback-enhances-positive); Butler and Roediger's delayed feedback came at the end of the test rather than after each item, which the widget could also do; this spec keeps per-item feedback.
5. **The review gap stays 21 days, now with a reason.** `SPACING_DAYS = 21` in `bin/lib/teach.mjs` (2026-11-01-pedagogy-spec-lines-at-research-vault) is kept and the reason recorded in the format: Cepeda et al. found "although there are costs to using a gap that is longer than the optimal value, these costs are much smaller than the costs of using too short a gap" and optimal gaps for recall of "approximately 3, 8, 12, and 27 days" at retention intervals of 7, 35, 70 and 350 days (2026-11-01-cepeda-vul-rohrer-wixted-pashler-2008); 21 days is long of those optima for retention intervals up to a few months and short of the 27-day optimum for a year. A per-mission horizon field was considered and dropped as a constant that would change behaviour only for a mission with a stated end date.

### D6. The page: measure, chrome, structure, status

1. **Measure.** Prose inside a block gets `max-width: 65ch`. "Width is no more than 80 characters or glyphs (40 if CJK)." is a mechanism WCAG 1.4.8 asks to be available, not a target width ("Content is not required to use these values.", 2026-11-01-w3c-2023-visual-presentation-understanding-sc); the practitioner range is "50–75 characters" with "around 70ch or 34em" as the CSS (2026-11-01-scott-2022-readability-optimal-line-length) and "45–90 characters, including spaces" (2026-11-01-butterick-line-length-butterick-s-practical). 65 rather than 70 because `ch` is "the advance measure of the glyph 0" (2026-11-01-mdn-css-length-data-type-units), and assuming the zero glyph is wider than the average glyph (0.56em against 0.5em, the assumption in 2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first) average text runs more characters than the number. By arithmetic the limit binds on spanning blocks, below 1100 px and, by about 6 px, on a half column at the 90rem cap; a body-grid half column is about 455 px at 1100 px and 625 px at the 90rem cap, against about 619 px for 65ch at 17 px (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first). Line height stays 1.65; the stylesheet has no `justify` rule (the snippet's probe) and none is added.
2. **Text spacing and resize survive.** The quoted stylesheet rules set no fixed heights, and the chip rule with its `white-space: nowrap` goes (next item), so an override of line height 1.5, paragraph spacing 2, letter spacing 0.12 and word spacing 0.16 times the font size (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-text-spacing-understanding-sc) and a 200% text resize (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-resize-text-understanding-sc) are expected to lose nothing; the implementation verifies both in a browser before the stylesheet ships.
3. **Citations are numbers, numbered the same way served and unserved.** Both pages number a citation by its position in the `sources` list; the served page adds the `href` and nothing else (today `footnoteCitations` numbers in "first appearance order" on the served page and the unserved page shows chips; both quoted in 2026-11-01-pedagogy-spec-lines-at-research-vault). `teach lint` checks an authored `[n]` against its source's position (`LESSON_CITE_NUMBER`). Unserved superscripts have no `href` and are styled muted without underline, so nothing looks like a link that is not one. The reason to drop the chips: "Every extra unit of information in an interface competes with the relevant units of information and diminishes their relative visibility." (2026-11-01-nielsen-2020-10-usability-heuristics-user) and "Avoid visual clutter: redundant links, irrelevant images, and meaningless typography flourishes slow users down." (2026-11-01-whitenton-2013-minimize-cognitive-load-maximize). The block labels stay as the cues that "highlight the" / "organization of the essential material." (2026-11-01-mayer-2020-multimedia-principles-one-page); each new block gets one (`complete` and `scaffold`: `do it yourself`, the step colour; `predict`: `first look`, the quiz colour; `connect`: `say it back`, the quiz colour) and a row in the span-or-pair list of the two-column grid (all four span). The `sources` block stays open and in place; a disclosure was considered and dropped, since progressive disclosure is for "advanced or rarely used features" (2026-11-01-nielsen-2006-progressive-disclosure-nn-g) and the sources are the lesson's evidence.
4. **Structure in the tree.** Every `[data-block]` section gets `aria-label` equal to its visible label, so the structure the labels convey is available in text: "Information, structure, and relationships conveyed through presentation can be programmatically determined or are available in text." (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-understanding-sc-1-3); the labels today are CSS `::before` content (lesson.css, quoted in the snippet) and none of the four built lessons has an `aria-label` on a `data-block` section (the snippet's probe). The `<mark>` in `complete` gets a colour token for both themes.
5. **The widget.** Each revealed option is prefixed with visible text, `Correct` or `Not this one`, since "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information" (2026-11-01-use-color-understanding-sc-1-4); `.quiz-why` and `.quiz-result` get `role="status"`, since status messages must be "programmatically determined through role or properties such that they can be presented to the user by assistive technologies without receiving focus" (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-understanding-sc-4-1); after an answer the options are marked `aria-disabled` rather than `disabled` (today `b.disabled = true`, quiz.js, quoted in the snippet), and each item is a `role="group"` labelled by its question; all of it stays operable from the keyboard (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-understanding-sc-2-1). By arithmetic over the quoted `.quiz-option` rule the buttons are about 49 px tall (2026-11-01-probes-after-spec-22-s-first), over the 24 by 24 CSS px floor (2026-11-01-w3c-2023-understanding-sc-2-5).
6. **Status.** `/lessons.json` gains the `PATH.md` line count and each lesson's line number and shape; the served navigation shows `line 4 of 10` and the index at `/` groups lessons under the project that closes their group and shows each shape: "The design should always keep users informed about what is going on" (2026-11-01-nielsen-2020-10-usability-heuristics-user). The score stays on `/progress`. The win sentence before `followup` (D1) is the closure: "Sequences of actions should be organized into groups with a beginning, middle, and end." (2026-11-01-shneiderman-2016-eight-golden-rules-interface).
