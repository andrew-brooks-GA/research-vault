# Five lesson-presentation issues from the wiki-quality research (2026-11-02)

One section per GitHub issue, gated here before posting, then one paragraph ordering the five. Each section says what the workspace does at research-vault 4e2dc2c, what the wiki evidence says, and what remains. The repo lines cited are quoted in snippet 2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-lesson; where a sentence states what code does, it is read from the quoted shape, not from a run. The wiki evidence is the notes behind synthesis 2026-11-02-what-makes-wiki-high-quality-technical, each cited by id, with that synthesis's caveat carried once here: Wikipedia and the ArchWiki are the two wikis with external quality evidence in the vault; Gentoo Wiki, Wikivoyage, Wookieepedia and the Old School RuneScape (OSRS) Wiki are candidate models judged by their own standards; Gwern.net is a single-author site cited as a design reference, not as a wiki. These five are research-teach issues and sit outside the twelve issues that commit 4e2dc2c ordered ("all twelve ordered by benefit; titles carry the rank", 2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-lesson); the ordering paragraph ranks them only among themselves and is judgment, not evidence.

## Issue A. Lesson lead: one sentence that states the idea, beside the win and the activity

What exists. The `mission` block holds "One sentence tying the win to `MISSION.md`, then in a shaped lesson one naming the activity" (LESSON-FORMAT.md line 23, 2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-lesson). The win is restated at the bottom: `followup` comes "after one sentence stating the win now held" (line 34). The unshaped fixture's mission is its win sentence, "This lesson gets you to predict what fx prints for a given input." (line 163; the fixture declares no `data-shape`, line 155), and the `tool` fixture's mission is the win sentence followed by the activity sentence "You will complete the command yourself." (test/fixtures/teach/lessons/0004-tool-fxctl.html line 12). Both say what the learner will be able to do; neither says what is true about the subject, which for a `concept` lesson is "a term set and the model behind it" (line 47) and first appears in the `model` block. The term a lesson introduces is defined in a `<dfn>` "where it is first explained" (line 10), and the term rule allows this lesson's own `data-introduces` terms anywhere in its prose (bin/lib/lesson.mjs lines 413 to 416). The served `/` renders an index of every lesson (bin/lib/teach.mjs lines 414 to 427 and 479), so a learner can open any lesson from the list.

What the wikis do. Wikipedia's Manual of Style says content should begin with a lead section that summarizes the article; its Featured article criteria ask for a concise lead that summarizes the topic; its technical-articles guideline asks that the lead be understandable without depending on links where this can be done (2026-11-02-wikipedia-quality-criteria-manual-style-technical). The OSRS Wiki generally opens an article with a lead summarizing the most important points, limited to a few paragraphs, for articles long enough to have one (2026-11-02-fan-wiki-style-guides-old-school). Every Page is Page One's topic characteristics include "establish their context", for readers who arrive "looking for the search box" (2026-11-02-documentation-structure-frameworks-diataxis-every-page).

What remains. In `concept`, `tool` and `diagnose` lessons, `mission` gains a third sentence, the idea: what is true about the subject, as distinct from the win (what the learner can do afterwards) and the activity (what they will do on the page). It uses only terms allowed before this lesson's `data-introduces`, describing the new thing rather than naming it ("a control plane that runs as pods inside another cluster's namespace", not the term), so the `<dfn>` stays in `model`. Review lessons are exempt: they are retrieval practice over records already written (line 124) and introduce no idea. `project` lessons are exempt: their `mission` already states the goal and the observable result that proves it (line 59). Built lessons gain the sentence at their next revision, since an edited `mission` unit needs a new claims-gate verdict. No new lint: whether a sentence states an idea is the reader's judgment (Issue E). Done when LESSON-FORMAT.md line 23 names the sentence, the shaped fixtures 0004-tool-fxctl.html and 0005-diagnose-fx.html carry it (no `concept` fixture exists, so the first built `concept` lesson revised after this lands is that case's check), and `npm test` passes.

## Issue B. A reading page: the lesson's conventions explained once, served and linked

What exists. Block labels are CSS generated content: a generic fallback at lesson.css line 69, then one rule per block at lines 71 to 99, with `fact` labelled twice (line 73, overridden at line 109); each block mirrors its label in an `aria-label` (LESSON-FORMAT.md line 18), and a coupling row ties the format's label column to the stylesheet (scripts/deps.mjs line 99). The command conventions live in the format for authors: in `example` a command is a `<pre>` line starting with `$ ` (line 88), in `step` every non-empty, non-comment `<pre>` line is a command (line 89), `complete` marks the withheld part with `<mark aria-label="withheld">____</mark>` (line 29), and `followup` is exactly "You can ask follow-up questions in the terminal." (line 34). The server serves the workspace's `lessons`, `reference` and `assets` directories (bin/lib/teach.mjs line 320) and the routes `/`, `/lessons.json`, `/progress` and `/progress.json` (lines 479 to 482); `indexHtml` renders an `index` section and, when reference docs exist, a `reference-index` section (lines 414 to 427); a served page turns each `data-cite` anchor into a numbered `[n]` link carrying the entry's title, numbered by the id's position in the `sources` list (`footnoteCitations`, lines 338 to 364, called at line 379); the navigation bar is built by quiz.js (lines 71 to 79); and a shaped lesson's `mission` names the activity (line 23). So the `[n]` links explain themselves and the activity sentence names the middle block's task, but nothing served explains in one place what a label such as "first look" or "say it back" asks of the learner, what the `$` prompt and the withheld mark are, or that `[n]` follows the order of the `sources` list.

What the wikis do. The ArchWiki's Help:Reading explains its conventions on one page: `#` versus `$` prompts, italic pseudo-variables, ellipses that in most cases mark omitted or optional text, "Install the foobar package" standing for the full procedure, and the link templates; most articles follow Don't Repeat Yourself and assume the reader will seek out supporting material they do not yet understand. Help:Style asks authors to link an article that already covers a procedure instead of duplicating it (2026-11-02-archwiki-style-conventions-why-it-praised). Every Page is Page One's topics "assume the reader is qualified", "establish their context" and "link richly" (2026-11-02-documentation-structure-frameworks-diataxis-every-page).

What remains. One file, `reading.html`, in the plugin's `skills/research-teach/assets/`, added to the `cp -n` line that copies the shared assets into a workspace (SKILL.md line 83), so it is served at `/assets/reading.html` with no new route. Step 4 runs on the first lesson in a workspace only (line 81), so an existing workspace would never receive the file; the step drops that restriction and runs the copy on every lesson build, which delivers a missing file at the next lesson and still leaves an edited copy alone, since `cp -n` never overwrites (line 86). It explains each block label and what the learner does in that block, the `$` prompt, the withheld mark, the `[n]` numbering and the follow-up reminder, in the lesson stylesheet. `indexHtml` links it from `/` (server side, so every workspace), and quiz.js's navigation bar links it (new workspaces, or an existing one after it refreshes quiz.js). A row in the `COUPLINGS` array of `scripts/deps.mjs` (line 28) ties the page to the label list, beside the row at line 99. No placeholder convention is introduced: a placeholder in a command would collide with the rules that `step` states the command exactly as the experiment's Result quotes it (line 39) and that `complete`'s filled command must match an `example` command (line 56). The lesson-reader is not told about the page: it reads only the lesson file (agents/lesson-reader.md line 10; test/lesson-reader.test.mjs line 23), as the learner might. Done when the file exists in the plugin's assets, SKILL.md step 4 copies it on every lesson build, `indexHtml` and quiz.js link it, the coupling row exists, and `npm test` passes.

## Issue C. Lint: time-relative words in lesson and reference prose

What exists. `bin/lib/lesson.mjs` declares seventeen `LESSON_` rules, from `LESSON_COMPLETE_NOT_WORKED` at line 300 to `LESSON_PLAN_NO_ELEMENTS` at line 444; `LESSON_TERM_NOT_INTRODUCED` tests tracked terms against the text `proseOf` collects, every block except `sources` and `followup` with `<pre>` and `<script>` removed plus the quiz items, and `LESSON_CLAIM_UNCITED` checks each `fact` paragraph for a citation (lines 228 to 237, 371 and 413 to 416); neither counts words; a grep for `words`, `sentence` or `wordCount` over the file matches the tool-word split at lines 288 and 289 and a comment at line 602, none a prose metric (2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-lesson). A reference doc stores no freshness date, because the note's `last_verified` is derived and a stale note surfaces through the session brief and `check` (LESSON-FORMAT.md line 128), and its `note` block is "The rendered vault note body" (line 35). So a page's currency is carried by the vault, and a word like "currently" in the prose asserts it a second time, silently, with no date.

What the wikis do. The ArchWiki's Help:Style asks for no "currently", beside avoiding contractions and, in general, the first person; its note records that many of its rules say "should" (2026-11-02-archwiki-style-conventions-why-it-praised). Gwern.net dates each page by the last time it was meaningfully modified (2026-11-02-gwern-net-long-content-perpetual-drafts); that reader-facing date is not proposed here, since line 128 keeps freshness in the vault, and this issue only removes the word that would contradict it.

What remains. `teach lint` gains an advisory `WARN_LESSON_TIME_WORD`: for each `<p>` in every lesson block except `sources`, and in a reference doc's `note` block, with text inside `<pre>` and `<code>` excluded, report "currently", "recently", "nowadays", "at the moment", "as of now" and "as of today", naming the block and quoting the paragraph's first words; no sentence splitting. The fix the warning names is a version or date taken from the cited entry; for a reference doc the fix lands in the vault note, which is then re-rendered, not in the page. A warning, not a failure, because a quoted source sentence may contain the word. Done when the rule exists in `bin/lib/lesson.mjs`, is documented beside the other lint rules, the fixture lessons under test/fixtures/teach/lessons/ and the built lessons are clean or fixed, and `npm test` passes.

## Issue D. `teach lint --prose`: sentences per block, average and longest

What exists. A grep for `words`, `sentence` or `wordCount` over `bin/lib/lesson.mjs` finds no prose metric (the grep above). `teach lint` prints `lint: clean` or one line per violation, then `warning` lines, and exits 1 on any violation (bin/commands/teach.mjs lines 49 to 53); two tests assert that exact stdout, `lint: clean\n` (test/teach.test.mjs lines 39 and 129), the workspace test expects exit 1 with every lesson violation on 0002-bad.html (lines 43 to 60), and SKILL.md step 6 loops "until it prints `lint: clean`" (line 88). Spec 22 D2 recorded "the measured growth of `fact` from 163 to 900 words (2026-11-01-measured-four-built-vcluster-lessons-prose)" (docs/specs/22-lesson-shapes.md line 48), so the project has measured prose only with an ad hoc script, first on 2026-11-01.

What the wikis do. Wikipedia's technical-articles guideline says "Use some short sentences and short paragraphs": comprehension decreases when average sentence length exceeds about 12 words, but too many short sentences in a row become monotonous, so vary sentence length (2026-11-02-wikipedia-quality-criteria-manual-style-technical). Gentoo asks for paragraphs of generally one to five sentences (2026-11-02-gentoo-wiki-guidelines-consistent-presentation-without). Gwern compares essays to code, "so many ways to err that you need a process with as much automation as possible", and runs a Markdown lint-checker over them (2026-11-02-gwern-net-long-content-perpetual-drafts).

What remains. A `--prose` flag on `teach lint`. With it, after the usual lines, one line per prose block (`mission`, `model`, `fact`, `example`, and a reference doc's `note`) of every lesson: `prose lessons/<file> <block>: <p> paragraphs, <s> sentences, avg <n> words, longest <m>`, where a sentence ends at `.`, `!` or `?` followed by a space or the end of the paragraph, and a word is a whitespace-separated token, over the same `<p>` text Issue C scans. Without the flag, stdout and the exit code are unchanged, so the tests at lines 39 and 129 and the step 6 loop are untouched. No threshold: the 12-word figure is Wikipedia's advice for a general audience and the project has no measurement of its own, so the line is a number for the reader round and the GATE-LOG record to argue from. Done when `teach lint --prose` over test/fixtures/teach prints one line for each prose block of 0001-good.html, `teach lint` without the flag prints what it prints today, and `npm test` passes.

## Issue E. lesson-reader: the frame's reading order, shape-neutral checks, a Lead check, words to avoid, why a command runs now, concrete examples

What exists. `agents/lesson-reader.md` line 12 has the reader read "the mission block, the fact block, the example block, the quiz, the step, the sources", while the frame is `prereq`, `mission`, the shape's middle, `sources`, `followup`, and the middles add `model`, `predict`, `complete`, `connect` and `scaffold` (LESSON-FORMAT.md lines 43 to 50). Its seven checks are Thread, Terms, Why, Example, Quiz, Step and Register (lines 16 to 22); Thread and Quiz are written against "the fact block" (lines 16 and 20), which `tool` and `project` lessons do not have (lines 48 and 50); Example asks whether output has "a sentence telling the reader what to notice" (line 19), which the format already requires of `example` commands (line 88); Step asks whether the reader will know what success looks like (line 21), over a block that holds the action, the command and the observable result (line 31); Register quotes lab-note phrases (line 22). SKILL.md dispatches the reader with a prompt that is exactly the lesson's path (line 92). Spec 22 D8 left "The lesson reader's checks are unchanged" because "an agent-file edit is not free" on account of the ledger's reviewer hash (docs/specs/22-lesson-shapes.md line 93); `reviewerHash` hashes `REVIEWER_FILE`, which names `agents/claims-reviewer.md` (bin/lib/gate.mjs lines 26 and 30, called at lines 140, 155 and 166), so, read from that shape, an edit to `agents/lesson-reader.md` does not change the hash the ledger rows are compared with, and this issue reverses D8 knowingly (all lines in 2026-11-02-lines-behind-2026-11-02-lesson).

What the wikis do. Wikipedia's Manual of Style asks for a lead that summarizes the article and says to avoid "note that", "of course" and "obviously"; its technical-articles guideline asks writers to write "one level down", expand acronyms and explain jargon at first use, and give concrete examples (2026-11-02-wikipedia-quality-criteria-manual-style-technical). Gentoo asks for a lead-in sentence before every operation such as an Emerge box (2026-11-02-gentoo-wiki-guidelines-consistent-presentation-without). On person: the OSRS Wiki asks for no "I", "we" or "me" because nobody owns articles, while its guides and instructional content that break the fourth wall prefer "you" where practical; Gentoo writes in the third person with no "you"; the ArchWiki asks for, in general, no first person (2026-11-02-fan-wiki-style-guides-old-school, 2026-11-02-gentoo-wiki-guidelines-consistent-presentation-without, 2026-11-02-archwiki-style-conventions-why-it-praised). The synthesis lists impersonal prose as a shared habit of those three (2026-11-02-what-makes-wiki-high-quality-technical); it is declined here: a lesson is instructional content that speaks to one learner directly, the fourth-wall case the OSRS rule reserves "you" for, and the shipped `example` label is itself first person, "the output, read with me" (LESSON-FORMAT.md line 27).

What remains, six text changes to `agents/lesson-reader.md`, no code:

1. Line 12 lists the frame's blocks in the order the page shows them, so the reader also reads `prereq`, `model`, `predict`, `complete`, `connect` and `scaffold`.
2. Thread (line 16) and Quiz (line 20) say "the model and fact blocks" instead of "the fact block", so they apply to every shape.
3. A Lead check before Thread: "After the mission block, can the reader say in one sentence what this page will teach? Quote the sentence that says it, or name its absence." Until Issue A lands, it is expected to report the absence where a mission holds only the win and the activity.
4. Register gains the words to quote: "note that", "of course" and "obviously", because Wikipedia's Manual of Style says to avoid them. Time-relative words go to Issue C's lint, not to the reader.
5. Example and Step gain one clause: "Name any command the page runs without a sentence before it saying why it is run now." What to look at in the output is already required by line 88 and checked at line 19; the "why now" is the new part.
6. Why gains: "Name the paragraph where a reader who knows only what the page says they know would need a concrete example to follow, and none is given."

Not added here: time words (Issue C); sentence length, which is a number and goes to `teach lint --prose` (Issue D); a first- or second-person rule (declined above); the reading page of Issue B, which the reader does not read, since line 10 and the test at test/lesson-reader.test.mjs line 23 keep it to the lesson file alone. Done when the agent file carries the six changes, `npm test` passes with line 23 of that test untouched, and a reader round over a copy of test/fixtures/teach/lessons/0004-tool-fxctl.html whose `mission` is reduced to its win sentence (line 12, before "You will complete the command yourself.") names the missing lead under Lead.

## Not proposed

Two recommendations of the synthesis are already met and get no issue: inline citations for factual claims and a sources list: every `fact` paragraph needs a citation (`LESSON_CLAIM_UNCITED`, bin/lib/lesson.mjs line 371) and every lesson carries a `sources` block of the ids cited (LESSON-FORMAT.md line 33); and a written process with both a quality target and a floor: on my reading, the pre-publication gates, `teach lint`, the reader gate and the claims gate (SKILL.md lines 90 to 94), play the floor's role and the reader's checks the target's, while Wikipedia's floor is deletion, which a single-learner workspace has no analogue of. Gwern's reader-facing page date is declined, as Issue C says, because LESSON-FORMAT.md line 128 keeps freshness in the vault.

## The order of the five

Judgment. Issue A first: one sentence in the format and two fixtures, no code, and the change every later reader round benefits from. Issue E second: one prompt file; its Lead check depends on A. Issue C third: one advisory lint rule over lesson `<p>` text. Issue B fourth: a page, two links and a coupling row, the largest surface. Issue D last: a flag and a readout with no threshold, useful only once there are rounds to compare.
